# Common Pitfalls

## Introduction

This guide aims to highlight common pitfalls encountered in the process of manuscript submission and review for scientific journals, specifically focusing on the Telemática journal. By understanding these pitfalls, authors and reviewers can ensure a smoother publication process and uphold the integrity and quality of scientific communication.

## Common Pitfalls for Authors

### Manuscript Structure and Submission

- **Accurate Reporting**: Authors must present a concise and accurate account of their work, ensuring an objective discussion of its significance.
- **Author Identification**: All authors should be registered with their unique ORCID identifiers and institutional affiliations, including geographic location and email addresses.
- **Contribution Specification**: The specific contributions of each author must be detailed using the CRediT taxonomy, which includes roles such as project management, data curation, and writing.
- **Conflict of Interest**: Authors must disclose any potential conflicts of interest in their research and communication.

### Ethical Considerations

- **Plagiarism and Fraud**: Manuscripts are checked for originality to prevent plagiarism and fraudulent practices. Any suspicion of unethical behavior will result in the withdrawal of the document from the editorial process.
- **Open Science Practices**: Authors are encouraged to use preprint servers and share underlying data to promote transparency and collaboration.

## Common Pitfalls for Reviewers

### Objective Evaluation

- **Quality Assessment**: Reviewers should judge manuscripts objectively, focusing on promoting effective scientific communication without personal criticism.
- **Conflict of Interest**: Reviewers must disclose any conflicts of interest and avoid cases where such conflicts could bias their evaluation.

### Ethical Responsibilities

- **Plagiarism Detection**: If reviewers suspect plagiarism or data falsification, they must confidentially inform the journal's management.
- **Intellectual Property**: The content of submitted articles is the property of the authors and should not be used or disclosed by reviewers without consent.

## Review Process

### Academic Review

- **Evaluation Rounds**: Manuscripts may undergo up to three evaluation rounds within four months, focusing on novelty, methodological quality, and ethical compliance.
- **Reviewer Recommendations**: Based on the evaluation, reviewers can recommend acceptance, rejection, or revisions.

## Summary

Avoiding common pitfalls in manuscript submission and review involves adhering to ethical guidelines, accurately reporting contributions, and maintaining transparency. Both authors and reviewers play crucial roles in upholding the integrity of scientific communication by following these best practices.

For more detailed guidelines and ethical standards, refer to the [Telemática journal's about page](https://revistatelematica.cujae.edu.cu/index.php/tele/about).
